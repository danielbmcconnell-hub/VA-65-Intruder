MILESTONE 2.1 ADDENDUM — DENTON, STOCKDALE, AND THE ALCATRAZ ELEVEN

Expand the POW captivity experience in Intruder: Yankee Station to incorporate the historically documented experiences of Jeremiah Denton, James Stockdale, George Coker, George McKnight, and the other members of the Alcatraz Eleven.

Preserve the existing Milestone 2 gameplay, flight missions, photography, and historical materials.

1. HISTORICAL CHARACTERS

Introduce historically accurate representations of Jeremiah Denton and James Stockdale within the POW storyline.

Denton was an A-6A Intruder pilot and commanding officer of VA-75 when he was shot down in July 1965.

Stockdale was a naval aviator shot down in September 1965 who subsequently became a senior leader of the American POW resistance.

Both were confined at Alcatraz along with George Coker and other American prisoners.

Use verified historical photographs when available.

Do not invent quotations and present them as authentic statements.

Clearly distinguish reconstructed dialogue from documented historical accounts.

2. THE ALCATRAZ ELEVEN

Develop an expanded Alcatraz prison chapter beginning in October 1967.

Reconstruct the arrangement of individual cells and the restrictive conditions described by former prisoners.

The player should gradually discover the identities of neighboring POWs through covert communication.

Historical prisoners should remain physically separated where appropriate.

Avoid presenting Alcatraz as an ordinary prison where inmates freely socialize.

3. JEREMIAH DENTON AND THE TAP CODE

Expand the existing POW tap-code system.

The player should learn how to communicate through a five-by-five alphabetic grid.

Introduce progressively more complicated messages, communication challenges, and meaningful exchanges with neighboring prisoners.

Allow messages to provide emotional support, historical context, information about prison conditions, and clues that may eventually contribute to another escape attempt.

Denton should be represented as an important figure in the prisoners' communication network, consistent with documented accounts.

4. DENTON'S MORSE-CODE INTERVIEW

Introduce an optional historical flashback depicting Denton's May 2, 1966, televised interview.

Allow the player to observe and decode the Morse-code message T-O-R-T-U-R-E.

Explain the significance of the message reaching American intelligence.

Use authentic archival footage or historically researched visual reconstruction where licensing permits.

This must be identified as a historical flashback, not an event the player personally witnesses during the 1967 escape.

5. JAMES STOCKDALE AND BACK US

Introduce Stockdale's documented BACK US guidance.

Incorporate its principles into prisoner communication, interrogation decisions, and the preservation of solidarity.

Create interactive experiences emphasizing leadership, moral dilemmas, communication, and the importance of maintaining the prisoners' chain of command.

Include references to Stockdale's Stoic philosophy and mental discipline.

Do not turn resistance to torture into an unrealistic test of reflexes or imply that suffering or forced compliance constitutes personal failure.

6. MENTAL AND PHYSICAL SURVIVAL

Integrate the guidance received from fellow prisoners with the existing mental-survival systems.

Continue the imaginary automobile, house, and city-building activities.

Add historical POW communication exercises, optional prayer, recollection of family memories, and the Boy Scout Oath and Law.

Visualize imagined environments as discussed in the main Milestone 2.1 requirements.

Make the completed exercises contribute meaningfully to progression through captivity.

7. RELEASE FROM SOLITARY AND FURTHER ESCAPE

After surviving appropriate periods of confinement, maintaining health and morale, and completing meaningful objectives, the player should have opportunities to return to regular imprisonment.

Returning to regular confinement should unlock another fully playable escape opportunity.

Information obtained through the prisoner communication network may provide clues about the environment or changes in guard routines.

Do not create an automatic escape or arbitrary success button.

Make subsequent attempts progressively understandable and achievable through actual gameplay.

Include optional guidance and checkpoints.

8. HISTORICAL ACCURACY

Research Denton, Stockdale, Coker, and McKnight using authoritative sources, including the Naval History and Heritage Command, National Archives, Department of Defense POW histories, and documented firsthand testimony.

Preserve historical chronology.

Do not place prisoners together in circumstances contradicted by the historical record.

Keep the documented history separate from fictional alternate endings.

Include a historical reference section allowing the player to learn more about these individuals.

9. TESTING

Verify that the player can learn and use the tap code, receive guidance, progress through solitary confinement, return to regular imprisonment, and attempt another escape.

Include automated tests that complete these sequences through genuine gameplay.

Test on Windows and simulated iPhone layouts.

Deliver an updated playable Netlify ZIP, screenshots, and test report.

Implement these changes within the ongoing Milestone 2.1 development effort.
