# Milestone 2.1 play guide

Choose **Ready Room → Enter the prison** to continue the fictional October 1967 escape chapter, or resume a POW checkpoint. Mission 11 still leads into the physical prison; capture on other missions still enters confinement. Flight missions and the original tribute photographs remain available.

For the original cell-to-rooftop, Hanoi and river route, use the [Milestone 2 play guide](MILESTONE_2_PLAY_GUIDE.md). The additions below continue that experience after recapture: learn the covert network, complete remembered projects, recover through confinement and physically attempt another escape. **History & photos** and the optional Morse lesson are also available without waiting for recapture.

## Photographs and historical context

**History & photos** includes the new Jeremiah Denton and James Stockdale references. Their supplied PNG bytes, color treatment, resolution and proportions are unchanged. Their user identification is recorded; exact archival provenance and frame dates are unverified. Captivity's **Context / log** has direct portrait links. The photo viewer pauses the game and restores its prior pause state; Escape closes the viewer.

**Denton Morse flashback · May 1966** is optional and separate from the 1967 chapter. The photograph stays static. A separate authored signal teaches Morse; it is not archival footage or synchronized blink reconstruction. Replay a letter, observe short and long pulses, enter dots/dashes, select the decoded letter and check it. Complete all seven letters to decode TORTURE. Replays and mistakes carry no condition penalty, and the flashback is never required for escape. Reduced presentation slows its signal.

## Separated cells and the prisoner network

Recapture enters a small, permanently lit solitary cell. Surrounding cells are separated schematic spaces; their layout and your reconstructed messages do not claim an exact historical cell order. The game begins named Alcatraz communication only in the late-October 1967–1969 context; earlier contact is anonymous. The conventional Eleven roster is documented in the historical audit, with remaining primary-source verification identified.

Inspect the wall using held F or touch **Interact**, then count row and column taps using the five-by-five C/K-sharing chart. Count the displayed incoming pulses, enter each pair, commit the letter and check the complete message. Correct exchanges gradually reveal identities, encouragement, leadership guidance and a guard-changeover clue. Transmit a correctly counted reply as well. Guards can investigate repeated sounds, interrupting the exchange; wait for the inspection to pass.

**Prisoner network** keeps the remembered identities and decoded messages. Photo links appear beside Denton, Stockdale and Coker once their identities are learned. BACK US matching uses a labeled conservative paraphrase: B/A/C/K, with US as Unity over Self. Its dilemmas let you reconnect, ask for recovery, distinguish facts from rumors or help a contact. No choice grades courage or forced compliance. Optional family-memory, Scout Oath/Law and reflection exercises use timed observation and ordered recall. Prayer or belief is never a progression requirement.

## Imagined projects in three dimensions

The Engine, House and City tabs each offer **Explore ... in 3D**. Walk and look around the imagined space using the existing first-person controls. Approach a component, structural option or city plot and hold F/Interact to act. Expand **Project instructions** when you need the component jobs, constraints or progress details; the panel folds away to keep the landscape visible. These actions use the same real dependency, budget and placement rules as the existing projects. They are imagined environments: your physical body stays in the cell.

The engine requires bearings before crankshaft, connected rotating parts, oil/cooling/ignition and drivetrain; then trace Intake, Compression, Power, Exhaust and recall the requested jobs. The house grows through foundation, frame, roof, services and furniture, with usable doorway and floor collision. The city needs connected streets, safe utilities, housing, school, park and transport; select a tool and physically visit plots before validating the plan. Completed builds stay visible in the remembered space.

**Return to cell** restores the physical position, scene, guards and lighting. Save preserves project/network progress and your physical surroundings; resuming a checkpoint returns you to the prison with those projects remembered. Movement in imagination does not make noise or move guards outside; actual project selections still consume the existing activity time and effort.

## Return to regular imprisonment

A fictional camp transfer becomes available after at least four simulated weeks, three different completed projects/memory activities, corridor observations on two different days, communication and adequate condition. During the named Alcatraz era, the network must also deliver its clue, receive a reply and record a thoughtful response and practice. Recover through meals/rest if needed. The door's context panel lists outstanding objectives.

Observe by approaching and facing the physical cell door, then holding F/Interact. If a pending interview appears, respond at your own pace and return to the cell; inspect the door again to record that day's observation. Repeated observation on the same day does not count toward the second occasion. Use the rest/sleep activities to maintain condition while the accelerated calendar advances.

Return to the cell, approach and face the door, then hold F/Interact on **Return to regular imprisonment**. This transfers you into another secure, explorable camp; it does not grant freedom.

Observe the cell's door slot. Supervised yard time is 16:00–18:00; calm movement there is permitted, while leaving at restricted times, contraband work and entering the workshop can draw pursuit. Find the concealed metal at the yard trough, watch the guards, work the workshop latch, remove three separate service-hatch fasteners, work the outside gate and physically cross the outer boundary. Release between held actions. The route continues onto the existing Hanoi street and river; there is no success roll or automatic rescue.

The remembered guard clue helps observation but never opens a barrier. Actual patrol schedules, sight, noise, collision and timing determine the attempt. A guard who has seen an escape attempt retains pursuit when you briefly return to a permitted area. Sustained close contact causes recapture and restores imprisonment; projects, identities and memories remain.

## Controls and checkpoints

W/S or Up/Down arrows move; A/D or Left/Right arrows turn (A/D strafe with mouse pointer lock). Hold Shift to run or C to crouch; Q/E looks vertically; Y enables mouse look. Hold F for nearby environmental work, release between actions. Space climbs at marked objects or dives in the river. Touch uses the left stick, right-side look drag, Run/Crouch, Interact and Climb/Dive. Escape closes an active history/lesson panel without opening another pause overlay. Settings provide normal/hard difficulty and standard/reduced presentation.

Save/Continue records browser-local progress, including regular-camp barriers, fasteners, conditions and the network. Saves are tied to the exact site and device; they do not automatically synchronize. The historical 1973 release ending remains separate from fictional subsequent escapes and rescue.

Read the [historical source audit](MILESTONE_2_1_HISTORICAL_SOURCES.md). No invented POW quotations, exact named adjacency, authentic film, archival photograph license, exact BACK US wording or clinical condition model is claimed.

Cloud checks use actual Chromium WebGL2 with desktop and emulated phone controls. Physical Windows/iPhone Safari, sustained device performance, available speech voices and human difficulty balance still need your trial. Prefer a separate Netlify site or retain previous deployments while comparing versions.
