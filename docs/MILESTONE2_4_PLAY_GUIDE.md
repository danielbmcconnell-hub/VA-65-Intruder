# Milestone 2.4 play guide

This update puts more emphasis on the bombardier/navigator's work: understanding the brief, finding a credible return, correcting navigation, making the designation and consenting through the computer's attack solution. It also makes real bomb impacts and target cook-offs easier to see, and records pistol ammunition expended during survival.

Use the updated hosted build in landscape on iPhone. Start with Medium graphics; choose Low if movement feels slow. The original eleven missions, carrier recovery, flight model, family photographs and playable POW chapters remain available.

## Begin in the right seat

Choose **Ready Room → Settings → Your seat → Right — B/N**. Return to operations and select **DIANE practice with guidance** to learn the attack controls, or select an existing combat mission. The game's existing pilot flies in B/N mode; you work navigation, designation, steering instructions and attack consent. DIANE practice retains its existing light AAA hazards.

Read the briefing before launch. Identify the assigned target, expected terrain or water pattern, ordnance and rules of engagement. Study the target-area chart and radar prediction. A bright return somewhere near the target area does not by itself identify the assigned target.

The optional **B/N worksheet** opens a compact, scrolling drawer during flight. Its recognition sketch is schematic, not a live target map. Close it whenever you need the full scope or cockpit view. It hides during teaching cards, the chart, pause, graphics-context loss and on-foot/POW gameplay. The existing **Flight guidance: ON/OFF** remains a separate optional coach.

## Work an attack

1. **Search and navigate.** Open **SCOPE**. Compare the coast, river and hard/soft returns with the briefing. Take the next real checkpoint fix before designation; watch **SYS ERR** decrease. A checkpoint LOCK updates navigation rather than selecting something to bomb.
2. **Compare the evidence.** Drag or slew the crosshairs onto a return. The worksheet's **CROSSHAIRS** readback samples the actual scope cells: bearing, range, fresh versus old sweep, and broad hard, concentrated hard, soft or weak return. It does not reveal a hidden target identity. Wait for a fresh sweep when an echo is old.
3. **Record your judgment if useful.** Select **My interpretation**, then choose **NOTE**. This records the position and your working hypothesis; it does not identify a contact, authorize weapons or award a score. A mistaken correlation can still lead to a mistaken attack.
4. **Designate deliberately.** Use **LOCK** in the worksheet, **Enter**, or the existing scope tap at your current crosshairs. The original targeting system acts on that position. Check the result: a named target, **MANUAL POINT**, an offset reference or briefed coordinates have different meanings. Follow the briefing's visual-identification requirement when applicable.
5. **Run the solution.** In B/N mode, **STEER/C** gives the existing pilot the crosshair steering instruction. Watch the original steering needle and **ASQ-61** solution. A valid designation is not yet a valid release solution. Settle the run and center the steering error.
6. **Consent through release.** Hold **COMMIT/Space** when cleared. The original computer chooses when the bombs leave; a quick tap is not a manual drop. Stores decrease only when the existing release occurs. One ripple releases per run; another pass requires the existing solution to re-arm.
7. **Observe and return.** Look up from the scope when visibility and the threat situation permit. Actual ground impacts now have clearer dust, flash and smoke. A destroyed fuel, ammunition or vehicle target can produce delayed secondary effects. Do not orbit under fire solely to watch an explosion. Turn feet wet, monitor fuel and use TACAN to recover.

The debrief reports original mission results, bombs expended, ARM launches where applicable, recorded miss distance and navigation fixes. If you made worksheet notes, it also shows the correlation history. A visible flash is not proof that every assigned target was destroyed. Worksheet notes belong to the currently loaded sortie; new POW checkpoints retain the ammunition ledger and recorded sortie context, rather than the worksheet's temporary notes.

When the worksheet's interpretation selector has keyboard focus, Space, Enter and arrows operate the form instead of the aircraft's weapons controls. Close the drawer or return focus to the game before using flight keyboard controls.

## Mission-specific decisions

**Depot, rail and bridge attacks:** use the briefed recognition pattern and surrounding geography. The worksheet gives recognition clues from the assigned mission, not a list of automatically identified targets.

**Concealed targets:** find the briefed hard offset aiming point. LOCK it, leave the crosshairs on that reference, then LOCK again to apply the existing bearing and distance offset. The worksheet does not turn concealed vehicles into visible radar targets.

**Blind weather attack:** no target echo confirms identity. Take the route fixes, designate the briefed ring and monitor system error. The original coordinate attack retains its navigation-error consequences.

**Weapons-tight surveillance:** identification occurs through the original overflight and photography requirements. Keep COMMIT released. A worksheet note does not complete reconnaissance or change clearance.

**Iron Hand:** the existing mission uses the **A-6B**, rather than adding ARM capability to an A-6A. Watch the actual emitter indication. A genuine ARM launch reduces the missile inventory and is counted separately from bombs. While a missile remains in flight, the worksheet shows observation guidance; emitter silence or a launch alone does not confirm a radar kill.

**Carrier practice:** no attack targets or stores are assigned. Follow the existing pattern, navigation and recovery instructions.

## Primary explosions and secondaries

The first flash comes from a real bomb or ARM impact. Secondary effects require actual destruction of an eligible target. Fuel storage billows darker smoke; ammunition and launchers can cook off in delayed bursts with sparks; vehicles and rolling stock have shorter sequences. Bridges and radar structures produce dust instead of invented fuel explosions. A miss can flash at its actual impact point without starting a target cook-off, and a water miss produces spray.

Effects are timed by the simulation, pause with the game and use bounded mobile budgets. They do not supply extra damage, extra kills or additional weapon releases. Visibility, weather, camera direction and distance still determine what you can see from the aircraft.

## Ammunition during survival

The **A-6A has no internal gun**. The new bullet counter concerns the existing carried survival revolver. Aircraft bombs and ARM missiles have separate expenditure records.

On foot, draw the revolver with **F** or **Pistol**, then attack with **Space** or **FIRE**. The survival HUD shows rounds remaining and **SPENT**. Every accepted pistol shot spends and counts one round, including a miss or a shot blocked by solid cover. An empty trigger, a holstered revolver, fists and knife attacks do not add bullets expended. The original cooldown and finite inventory remain in force.

Some existing first-contact choices describe ammunition consumption rather than a physical trigger shot. Their actual inventory change is included in the total and distinguished as first-contact rounds in the debrief. This avoids presenting a narrative choice as a shot visibly fired in the 3D scene.

Capture confiscates the revolver and carried rounds. It does not erase the rounds already expended. Save in the POW chapter, then use **Continue POW checkpoint** or an appropriate **Saved chapters → Resume** entry to retain the ledger. New checkpoints also preserve the original sortie's mission, aircraft stores, recorded target results and capture/ejection context, so resuming captivity does not relabel that flight as another mission. These are captivity checkpoints, not airborne flight saves.

The debrief and Cruise Log show the recorded pistol expenditure. Existing older log entries without ammunition records cannot reconstruct shots that were never saved.
