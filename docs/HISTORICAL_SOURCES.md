# Milestone 2 historical source audit

The preserved historical episode is the 12 October 1967 escape of Navy Lieutenant George Thomas Coker and U.S. Air Force Captain George G. McKnight. They traveled approximately 15 miles downstream and were recaptured. Reaching the delta, obtaining a boat, contacting a Navy vessel, freedom and rescue in the game are explicitly fictional alternate outcomes.

## Material actually read

- User-supplied `Coker Interview Summary.docx`, retained unchanged in `reference_media/coker_interview/`. A readable extraction is in `docs/historical_sources/coker_interview_summary.md`. Despite its repeated use of “transcript,” the supplied file is a mixture of summaries, quoted fragments, duplicate passages, damaged sentences and overlapping timestamps. The document itself provides no interview URL; the user subsequently supplied two source-video links, recorded below. The user clarified that Gemini generated the summary. It is treated as an AI-generated secondary account, not a verified primary transcript; the game uses paraphrased themes and does not attribute invented dialogue to Coker.
- The user's historical requirements, including the October 1967 date, McKnight's Air Force rank, approximate three-by-nine-foot solitary cell, the historical downstream distance and recapture. These are distinguished from independently verified archival facts.
- `CODEX_INTEGRATION_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, `README_FOR_CODEX.txt`, and `ASSET_MANIFEST.json` from the supplied photograph package. Its WebPs and PNG masters are preserved byte for byte.

The summary supports inspiration for scrap-metal lock preparation behind a hinge, picked handcuffs/covert siesta communication, observation of a circuitous route, an unscheduled inspection, travel from approximately 10 p.m. toward the river by midnight, a roughly three-knot current/five-hour swim, a mudbank hiding place discovered by a fisherman, and communication through tap patterns, sweeping, coughs and shadows. It emphasizes recovery after coercion, humane leadership, mutual encouragement, birthdays/anniversaries, optional faith, detailed mental construction and a personal oath as coping aids. Game puzzles and numerical condition meters are abstractions, not assessments of actual POW courage or medical/psychological accuracy.
