# Milestone 2.3 play guide

Open the updated hosted game and choose **Enter Ready Room → Enter the prison** for the improved cell-to-river escape. **Continue POW checkpoint** resumes your latest unfinished local save; **Saved chapters** offers the named chapter saves. Use landscape orientation on iPhone and start with Medium graphics, or Low if movement feels slow.

The existing **Solitary survival**, **Second escape**, **Red River journey** and flight mission entries remain available. Standalone practice does not award campaign qualifications. This guide covers the newly polished original escape; see the [Milestone 2.2 guide](MILESTONE_2_2_PLAY_GUIDE.md) for the later escape approaches, captivity routine and B/N coaching.

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard / mouse | Touchscreen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move | W/S or Up/Down arrows | Left stick |
| Turn / look | A/D or Left/Right arrows; Q/E looks vertically; Y enables mouse look | Drag the right side |
| Move quietly | Hold C to crouch | Crouch control |
| Run | Hold Shift | Run control |
| Environmental work | Face the object and hold F; release between actions | Face the object and hold **Interact**; release between actions |
| Throw a collected stone | R | **Throw stone** control |
| Climb / dive | Space at an appropriate object / hold Space in water | **Climb** / **Dive** |

Use the center reticle to face an object and watch its prompt. Work requires both reach and direction; a nearby object is not necessarily the current target. The working hands, tool and progress bar follow the real action. Opening menus pauses that action. **Settings & controls → Guard sound / sight cues** adds contextual hints based on actual actors. Guard dialogue is reconstructed game dialogue.

## Escape through the prison

Examine the cell before attempting the door. Search the bunk for the concealed bracket, communicate through the wall using the counted tap-code activity, and observe the corridor. The cell floor also has **three loose stones**: collect them through the normal held interaction. This is a finite supply.

A throw travels along your current aim. Walls and ceilings can stop it; the sound comes from its impact rather than the target you intended. Throw through an open doorway toward a useful location only when that location is physically reachable. Guards may investigate a sound, but a distraction does not erase a guard's direct view of you or evidence already discovered.

When the guard moves away, face the door fitting and hold Interact/F to loosen it. The visible bolt moves during work, but the closed door still blocks you until the action finishes. Releasing the control abandons unfinished work. After opening the door, **cover fresh marks on the loose fitting** if you have time. Concealment cannot make a guard forget marks already seen.

Reachable lamp switches are ordinary environmental objects. Face the switch and hold Interact/F to turn its light off or on. Darkness helps concealment, but it does not make close contact or noisy work safe. Listen and observe before leaving the cell. Crouched movement on the corridor and stairs is quieter than running; a guard already pursuing you can follow toward the roof.

## Reach and travel the Red River

Enter the water through actual movement. Swimming, floating with the current, diving, breath, fatigue and daylight remain active systems. Use the water's **Float / swim** interaction to switch modes. Swim to steer your path; floating lets the current carry you while you recover some effort. Diving is temporary because breath limits require resurfacing. Reeds, mudbanks and sheltered landings offer places to conceal yourself and rest when physically reached.

The new ripples and reflections show flowing water; they do not replace the existing current or change the collision surface. Patrols can still discover an exposed swimmer. Travel toward the delta and later opportunities depends on movement and condition, rather than a historical recapture timer. Later boat, Gulf and rescue outcomes remain difficult fictional alternatives to the documented ending.

## Return to remembered projects

The existing captivity activities still require actual component order, construction dependencies, spatial memory, city planning or counted communication. Completing automobile parts, house stages and city projects now adds corresponding visual details to their imagined spaces. The warmer imagined scenes are separate from the physical cell; leaving an activity returns you to your saved physical position.

Rest, optional faith, family recollection and other coping activities remain available. Resistance is not a reflex contest, and suffering or forced compliance is not presented as failure of courage. The reduced-intensity narrative setting remains in **Settings & controls**.

## Save and compare the build

Use **Save** before an uncertain attempt. Saves are local to the exact website origin, browser and device; they do not synchronize between Windows and iPhone. **Enter the prison** begins a new campaign. Use the chapter's **Resume** button to preserve an existing run.

The updated deployed browser title should say **Milestone 2.3**. A delivered ZIP does not itself update [the existing game address](https://steady-croquembouche-46c1a0.netlify.app/); follow the [release report's deployment steps](MILESTONE2_3_REPORT.md#update-the-existing-netlify-game).

The inspiration remains the **October 12, 1967** escape of Navy Lieutenant George Coker and U.S. Air Force Captain George G. McKnight, whose attempt ended in recapture. Layouts, schedules, dialogue, interactive tools and successful alternate outcomes are fictional. Historical photographs and William S. McConnell's tribute photographs are preserved. Cloud browser tests use Chromium WebGL2 and simulated touch layouts; physical Windows and iPhone Safari performance and difficulty still need your trial.
