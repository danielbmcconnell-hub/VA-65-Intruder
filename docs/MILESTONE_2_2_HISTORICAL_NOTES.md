# Milestone 2.2 historical boundaries

This update changes gameplay and controls. It makes no new claims of historical research. The [Milestone 2.1 source audit](MILESTONE_2_1_HISTORICAL_SOURCES.md) remains the authority for sources actually retrieved, unresolved assertions and the supplied photographs. The Gemini-generated interview summary remains secondary inspiration rather than a verified transcript.

The Coker–McKnight inspiration is October 12, 1967. George G. McKnight was a U.S. Air Force captain. Their documented attempt ended in recapture. The original flight missions and all historical and family photographs retain their earlier bytes and captions.

The washing passage, workshop approach, covers, hinges, patrol schedules, cues, river landmarks, skiff/rescue possibilities and difficulty tuning are authored gameplay. They are not a surveyed reconstruction of Dirty Bird or a documented route through Hanoi. Later escape success is fictional. Direct solitary, second-escape and river entry are explicitly labeled standalone practice; their dates support gameplay and do not establish an individual's presence in a particular cell.

Guard Vietnamese speech and translations, flight coaching and debrief advice are authored game text. They are not quotations from historical people or real aviation instruction. Directional guard hints depend on sight and audible movement, not historical testimony. Improved photographic motion uses the existing artwork; no new archival photographs or walking poses were generated.

Earned time compression represents an established routine, pauses for opportunities, and supplies neither decoded messages nor project answers. Activity effects, deadlines, transfer qualifications and condition scores remain fictional. The game does not grade a prisoner's courage or treat forced compliance as personal failure. Faith activities remain optional.
