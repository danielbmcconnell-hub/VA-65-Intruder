# Willard Egress — play guide

Open the deployed game, enter the Ready Room, and select **12 · WILLARD EGRESS — Night Interception, North Vietnam, 1966**. It is unlocked independently of the earlier missions. Choose a difficulty, leave the optional bombing pass enabled or disable it, then press **PLAY MISSION**. You begin airborne; there is no carrier launch to repeat.

This mission puts you in the **pilot's seat**, even if your normal preference is Right — B/N. You fly manually while the B/N supplies navigation and threat calls. Your existing seat preference remains available for the other missions. The A-6A has no internal gun: this is an escape, not a fighter duel.

## Controls

| Action | Windows keyboard/mouse | iPhone, landscape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pitch / bank | W/S and A/D; arrows also work outside the scope | Existing virtual flight stick |
| More / less power | Hold Shift / Ctrl; mouse wheel also adjusts power | Hold THR + / THR − |
| Check behind | H or **LOOK AFT**; press again to look forward | **LOOK AFT**, then **LOOK FORWARD** |
| Cockpit / chase view | V or **VIEW** | **VIEW** |
| Pause / resume | Esc; choose **Resume** | **PAUSE**; choose **Resume** |
| Retry saved stage | **CHECKPOINT**, or pause/result retry button | Same buttons |
| Radar / designate | Tab; arrows slew, X steps returns, Enter locks | **SCOPE**, drag crosshairs, **LOCK** |
| Attack consent | Hold Space through the computed release | Hold **COMMIT** |
| Gear and hook | G | **GEAR** |
| Eject | J | Original two-tap **EJECT** confirmation |

With normal pitch settings, W raises the nose and S lowers it. Existing invert settings still apply. While the scope is open, arrows slew the radar and WASD continues flying the aircraft. Briefly check aft, then return forward before maneuvering near the ground.

On iPhone, begin in landscape and try Medium or Low graphics. Tap PLAY or another normal control to enable browser audio. The new buttons are placed around the existing stick, throttle and COMMIT controls. Actual iPhone Safari performance still needs device testing.

## Fly the five phases

1. **The night strike.** Fly the assigned northern run-in. If you want to attack, use the existing radar, lock and ASQ-61 consent workflow with four Mk 82s. Bombing is optional: continuing past the strike also advances the mission. Disabling the pass starts with no bombs and a short playable departure segment.
2. **Enemy interceptor.** Listen to the dramatized B/N warning and use LOOK AFT. Contact information is approximate and can go stale; the display is not an omniscient fighter radar. Keep the Intruder moving while identifying the threat.
3. **Defensive flight.** Use changes of bank, height and heading rather than holding a steady target course. The MiG is more agile, but must turn, accelerate, regain sight and establish a cannon solution. Watch airspeed, radar altitude and damage. Pulling continuously into a stall or diving without ground clearance will end the sortie.
4. **The Willard Egress.** Follow the B/N course toward the Nam Dinh defense zone. Lead the pursuer into the belt, then change direction as the gun crews commit their fire. Their shells have travel time and remain hostile to your aircraft. Avoid sustained straight flight through the bursts. A useful break changes the MiG's approach while giving its pursuer a reason to react to nearby flak.
5. **Feet wet.** A break-off call is not the finish. Continue toward the indicated coastal egress with both crew members alive until sufficient separation and the safe naval approach are reached. Waiting, reaching a coordinate early or merely entering the gun belt does not award victory.

The normal successful route uses hostile flak to disrupt pursuit. Genuine prolonged terrain masking or a simulated interceptor terrain collision can produce an explicitly alternate evasion outcome; neither earns the flak-maneuver reward or establishes a historical kill.

## Checkpoints, difficulty and results

Automatic checkpoints are made after the night strike and before the gun-belt phase. **CHECKPOINT**, the pause menu and the result screen retry the latest valid saved stage. The briefing's **Continue Willard checkpoint** resumes it after a reload. Saves belong to this browser and website address; they are separate from the POW campaign saves.

A retry restores recorded aircraft condition, stores, mission/combat state and target results. It clears rounds already in flight and gives three seconds of protection from the new fighter/flak damage so you can regain control. Terrain is still dangerous. Retry penalties accumulate; repeatedly restarting does not erase them. An invalid save cannot overwrite the previous good checkpoint.

| Difficulty | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| **Rookie** | Existing assisted-flight handling for this mission, slower/less accurate enemy attacks and more forgiving damage. |
| **Naval Aviator** | Standard pursuit and gun profiles; uses your normal flight-assistance preference. Start here after learning the controls. |
| **Ace** | Faster enemy reactions, tighter cannon spread, stronger pursuit/damage and less distance assistance. Uses your normal flight-assistance preference. |

The score rewards escape, crew survival, aircraft integrity, avoiding prolonged stalls, the genuine flak maneuver and navigation to safety. It does not require shooting down the interceptor. The automated standard runs lasted approximately **8 minutes 13 seconds** and **8 minutes 30 seconds**; human completion time and difficulty will vary.

**MISSION COMPLETE** at the safe approach means successful egress, not a carrier landing. Choose **Continue carrier recovery** to fly the existing TACAN, gear/hook, glideslope and arresting-wire recovery. That continuation is optional. A recovered-aboard result requires real deck contact and a wire; the earlier safe-egress log is updated rather than duplicated.

If you eject, the existing parachute, ground survival, capture and POW systems remain available. Ejection does not count as successfully returning the aircraft and both crew in this mission.

## Historical context

The supplied account concerns VA-65, USS Constellation, pilot LT R. S. Williams and B/N LT J. E. Diselrod near Nam Dinh in 1966. Independent archival corroboration was unavailable during implementation. The pursuer's type is unconfirmed; the supplied MiG-17PF artwork is an interpretive representation. Crew dialogue, geography, combat and outcomes are authored reconstruction. [Historical source audit](WILLARD_EGRESS_HISTORY.md).

The original McConnell dedication, earlier flight missions and POW chapters remain separate. [Implementation and test report](WILLARD_EGRESS_REPORT.md).
