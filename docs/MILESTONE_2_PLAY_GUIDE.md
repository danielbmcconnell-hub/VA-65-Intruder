# Milestone 2 play guide

Open the hosted Netlify site, choose **Enter Ready Room**, then **Enter the prison** in the Dirty Bird card. **Continue POW checkpoint** resumes an unfinished local save. Mission 11's existing flight and capture also enter this chapter. Original missions remain selectable below the chapter card.

## Controls

- W/S or arrow up/down: forward/back; A/D or arrow left/right: turn.
- Shift: run; C: crouch. Running and noisy work attract attention.
- Q/E: look up/down. Y enables mouse look; with pointer lock, A/D strafe.
- Hold F near and facing an object. The displayed progress bar shows work duration. Release between interactions.
- Space: rope climbing; in water, hold to dive. Breath limits force surfacing.
- Touch: left stick moves/turns, drag the right side to look, use Run/Crouch and the contextual Interact/Dive buttons.
- Save records position, equipment, conditions, chapter progress and guard memory on this browser/device. Browser storage can be cleared; saves do not transfer automatically between Windows and iPhone.
- Settings & controls selects normal/hard difficulty and standard/reduced narrative intensity.

## Physical route

Inspect the bunk, observe the corridor and communicate through the wall. Concealed scrap metal lets you work the door hardware; noisy work can draw a guard to investigate. Walk out when the corridor is clear. Turn right toward the stairs, climb onto the roof, find a rope, secure it to the east parapet and descend. Walk east along the dark Hanoi lane while avoiding observers and patrols. The riverbank is the first major checkpoint.

The river continues through swimming, current, breath, fatigue, concealment and approaching daylight. Stay near reeds and mudbanks when watched. Reach shelter physically to rest. Travel, rather than a menu or fixed historical timer, advances the route toward the delta. A small working boat and an offshore signaling opportunity support a difficult fictional rescue branch. The historical fifteen-mile comparison never forces recapture.

## Captivity

Mental activities require component order, construction dependencies, spatial memory, connected city planning, or actual counted row/column taps. Optional faith and personal codes provide coping choices; rest and recovery remain valid choices. Nighttime shackles restrict physical movement.

Days, weeks and months bring changes in health, guard routines, food, interviews, contacts and possible transfer. An established monthly routine accelerates the calendar, with explicit time compression. Later escape preparation still leads back into the physical compound, where movement and evasion are required.
